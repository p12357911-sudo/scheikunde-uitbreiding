### nk3h-schakelingen-KB
heading Circuits
0.q How is an ammeter placed in a circuit?
0.a In series with the component
1.q How is a voltmeter placed in a circuit?
1.a In parallel with the component
2.q What is Ohm's law?
2.a U = I × R
3.q A current of 0.50 A flows through a resistor of 12 Ω. What is the voltage?
3.a U = I × R = 0.50 × 12 = 6.0 V
4.q The voltage across a resistor is 9.0 V and the current is 0.30 A. What is the resistance?
4.a R = U / I = 9.0 / 0.30 = 30 Ω
5.q A 46 Ω heating element is connected to 230 V. What is the current?
5.a I = U / R = 230 / 46 = 5.0 A
6.q What is the unit of resistance?
6.a Ohm (Ω)
7.q What is true for the current in a series circuit?
7.a The current is the same everywhere
8.q What is true for the voltage in a series circuit?
8.a The source voltage is shared between the components
9.q What is true for the voltage across components connected in parallel?
9.a The voltage across each component is the same
10.q What is true for the current in a parallel circuit?
10.a The total current is the sum of the currents through the branches
11.q What happens to the other lamps if one lamp burns out in a series circuit?
11.a All the lamps go out
12.q What happens to the other lamps if one lamp burns out in a parallel circuit?
12.a The other lamps stay on
13.q How are the appliances in a house connected?
13.a In parallel
14.q Two resistors of 10 Ω and 15 Ω are in series. What is the equivalent resistance?
14.a R<sub>v</sub> = R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub> = 10 + 15 = 25 Ω
15.q Three resistors of 8.0 Ω are in series. What is the equivalent resistance?
15.a R<sub>v</sub> = 3 × 8.0 = 24 Ω
16.q Two resistors of 6.0 Ω and 3.0 Ω are in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance? (1/R<sub>v</sub> = 1/R<sub>1</sub> + 1/R<sub>2</sub>)
16.a 1/R<sub>v</sub> = 1/6.0 + 1/3.0 = 1/2.0, so R<sub>v</sub> = 2.0 Ω
17.q Two equal resistors of 20 Ω are in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance?
17.a With two equal resistors, R<sub>v</sub> is half: 10 Ω
18.q What happens to the equivalent resistance if you connect an extra resistor in parallel?
18.a The equivalent resistance becomes smaller
19.q Two resistors of 10 Ω and 15 Ω are in series on a 12 V source. What is the current?
19.a R<sub>v</sub> = 25 Ω; I = U / R<sub>v</sub> = 12 / 25 = 0.48 A
20.q The same circuit: 10 Ω and 15 Ω in series on 12 V, current 0.48 A. What is the voltage across the 10 Ω resistor?
20.a U = I × R = 0.48 × 10 = 4.8 V
21.q A 6.0 Ω resistor and a 12 Ω resistor are in parallel on 12 V. What is the total current?
21.a I<sub>1</sub> = 12 / 6.0 = 2.0 A; I<sub>2</sub> = 12 / 12 = 1.0 A; I<sub>tot</sub> = 3.0 A
22.q In a parallel circuit, two ammeters in the branches read 0.30 A and 0.20 A. What is the total current?
22.a 0.30 + 0.20 = 0.50 A
23.q In a series circuit, two voltmeters across two lamps read 4.0 V and 2.0 V. What is the source voltage?
23.a 4.0 + 2.0 = 6.0 V
24.q You connect two 1.5 V batteries in series. What is the total voltage?
24.a 1.5 + 1.5 = 3.0 V
25.q A 6.0 V lamp has a power of 3.0 W. What is the current through the lamp? (P = U × I)
25.a I = P / U = 3.0 / 6.0 = 0.50 A
26.q How does the resistance of a thin wire compare with that of a thick wire (same material and length)?
26.a The thin wire has a greater resistance
27.q What happens to the resistance of a wire if you make the wire twice as long?
27.a The resistance becomes twice as large
28.q What does an NTC do at a higher temperature?
28.a Its resistance becomes smaller
29.q What does an LDR do when more light falls on it?
29.a Its resistance becomes smaller
30.q What do you use a variable resistor for?
30.a To control the resistance, and with it the current
31.q What happens in a short circuit?
31.a A very large current flows because the resistance is very small
32.q Three equal resistors of 30 Ω are in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance?
32.a With three equal resistors, R<sub>v</sub> is a third: 30 / 3 = 10 Ω
33.q Where do you connect a voltmeter to measure the voltage across a lamp?
33.a In parallel with the lamp (connected to both sides of the lamp)
34.q Name two advantages of a parallel circuit in a house.
34.a Every appliance gets the full mains voltage and you can switch appliances on and off separately
### nk3h-straling-KB
heading Radiation
0.q Which three types of radiation are released in radioactive decay?
0.a Alpha radiation, beta radiation and gamma radiation
1.q What does an alpha particle consist of?
1.a Two protons and two neutrons (a helium nucleus)
2.q What does a beta particle (beta-minus) consist of?
2.a A fast electron
3.q What is gamma radiation?
3.a Electromagnetic radiation with a lot of energy
4.q Which material already stops alpha radiation?
4.a A sheet of paper
5.q Which material stops beta radiation?
5.a A thin aluminium plate
6.q Which material is suitable for shielding gamma radiation?
6.a Thick lead or concrete
7.q Which type of radiation is the most penetrating?
7.a Gamma radiation
8.q Which type of radiation has the greatest ionising effect?
8.a Alpha radiation
9.q What does ionising radiation mean?
9.a Radiation that can knock electrons out of atoms or molecules
10.q Which radiation is ionising?
10.a X-rays
11.q Which radiation has the most energy?
11.a Gamma radiation
12.q What is the unit of activity (number of decays per second)?
12.a Becquerel (Bq)
13.q What is the unit of radiation dose in the body (a measure of the damage caused by radiation)?
13.a Sievert (Sv)
14.q A radioactive source has an activity of 500 Bq. How many atomic nuclei decay per second?
14.a 1 Bq = 1 decay per second, so 500 per second
15.q What is the half-life?
15.a The time in which half of the radioactive nuclei have decayed
16.q 80 g of a radioactive substance is present. The half-life is 4.0 days. How many grams are left after 12 days?
16.a 12 days = 3 half-lives: 80 → 40 → 20 → 10 g
17.q A source has an activity of 100 Bq. What is the activity after 3 half-lives?
17.a 100 → 50 → 25 → 12.5 Bq
18.q Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5730 years. What percentage of an amount of C-14 is left after 11 460 years?
18.a 11 460 years = 2 half-lives: 100% → 50% → 25%
19.q 1/8 of a substance is left. How many half-lives have passed?
19.a 1/2 → 1/4 → 1/8: three half-lives
20.q Radium-226 (atomic number 88) decays by emitting an alpha particle. What is the atomic number of the daughter nucleus?
20.a An alpha particle has 2 protons: 88 − 2 = 86
21.q Radium-226 (atomic number 88) decays by emitting an alpha particle. What is the mass number of the daughter nucleus?
21.a An alpha particle has mass number 4: 226 − 4 = 222
22.q Carbon-14 (atomic number 6) decays by beta-minus decay to nitrogen (atomic number 7). What happens to the mass number?
22.a A neutron changes into a proton: the mass number stays the same, the atomic number goes up by 1
23.q What is background radiation?
23.a Radiation that is always present around us, for example from the ground and from space
24.q What is the difference between irradiation and contamination?
24.a In irradiation you are hit by radiation from outside; in contamination there is radioactive material on or in your body
25.q How do you reduce the radiation exposure from a radioactive source? Name the three ways.
25.a Increase the distance, use a shorter time, use shielding
26.q What do you use to measure radioactive radiation?
26.a A Geiger counter
27.q What do you use X-rays for in a hospital?
27.a To make images of bones and organs
28.q Why is nuclear waste dangerous for a long time?
28.a Some substances have a very long half-life and so keep emitting radiation for a long time
29.q What happens in nuclear fission?
29.a A heavy atomic nucleus splits into smaller nuclei and energy is released
30.q Which radiation does a microwave oven use?
30.a Microwaves
31.q A smoke detector often contains a small source that emits alpha radiation. Why is that not dangerous as long as the detector is intact?
31.a Alpha radiation is already stopped by the casing and a thin layer of air
32.q Why is an alpha source especially dangerous if the radioactive material gets inside your body?
32.a Alpha radiation is strongly ionising and is completely absorbed in the body
33.q Name two applications of radioactive radiation.
33.a For example medical imaging (tracer), irradiation of tumours, sterilisation of medical equipment or preserving food
34.q What is the difference between ionising and non-ionising radiation? Give an example of each.
34.a Ionising radiation can ionise atoms (for example X-rays or gamma radiation); non-ionising radiation cannot (for example radio waves or visible light)

heading Fractions, percentages and units
0.q Write 11/3 as a mixed number.
0.a 3 2/3
0.e 11/3 = 3 and 2/3
1.q Write 5/8 as a decimal.
1.a 0.625
1.e 5 : 8 = 0.625
2.q Write 9/25 as a decimal.
2.a 0.36
2.e 9 : 25 = 0.36
3.q Write 0.6 as a simple fraction.
3.a 3/5
3.e 0.6 = 600/1000 = 3/5
4.q Write 3/8 as a percentage.
4.a 37.5%
4.e 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
5.q Simplify (6a)/(9a).
5.a 2/3
6.q Which fraction is greater: 5/7 or 7/10?
6.a 5/7
6.e 5/7 = 50/70 and 7/10 = 49/70, so 5/7 is greater
7.q What is 0.79 g / 1 mL?
7.a 0.79 g/mL
8.q You multiply a density of 0.79 g/mL by a volume of 50 mL. What unit does the answer have?
8.a g
9.q What does a molar mass of 18 g/mol mean?
9.a 1 mol has a mass of 18 g
9.e You read g/mol as "grams per mole": 1 mol has a mass of 18 g
10.q You divide a mass in g by a molar mass in g/mol. What unit does the answer have?
10.a mol
11.q You divide an amount in mol by a concentration in mol/L. What unit does the answer have?
11.a L
12.q 0.4 L of solution contains 0.15 mol of dissolved substance. Calculate the concentration in mol/L. Round to 3 decimal places.
12.a 0.375 mol/L
12.e 0.15 mol : 0.4 L = 0.375 mol/L
13.q Write 0.6% as a decimal.
13.a 0.006
13.e 0.6% = 0.6/100 = 0.006
14.q Write 0.375 as a percentage.
14.a 37.5%
14.e 0.375 × 100% = 37.5%
15.q A quantity of 60 g increases by 35%. How many grams is it afterwards? You do not need to round.
15.a 81 g
15.e 60 g × 1.35 = 81 g
16.q How much is 12.5% of 80 g?
16.a 10 g
16.e 12.5% = 1/8: 80 g : 8 = 10 g
17.q How much is 0.5% of 3000 mL?
17.a 15 mL
17.e 0.5% = 0.005: 0.005 × 3000 mL = 15 mL
18.q A mixture consists of 28 g of iron and 16 g of sulfur. What is the mass ratio iron : sulfur?
18.a 7 : 4
18.e 28 : 16 = 7 : 4 (divide both numbers by 4)
19.q Convert 3750 mL to L. Round to 1 decimal place.
19.a 3.8 L
19.e 1 L = 1000 mL, so 3750 mL = 3.75 L, rounded 3.8 L
20.q Convert 480 L to m<sup>3</sup>. You do not need to round.
20.a 0.48 m<sup>3</sup>
20.e 1 m<sup>3</sup> = 1000 L, so 480 L = 0.48 m<sup>3</sup>
21.q Convert 0.25 h to s. You do not need to round.
21.a 900 s
21.e 1 h = 3600 s, so 0.25 h = 900 s
22.q How many grams is 1 mg?
22.a 0.001 g
22.e 1 g = 1000 mg, so 1 mg = 0.001 g
23.q A speed of 36 km/h in m/s is equal to …
23.a 10 m/s
23.e 36 km/h = 36 000 m : 3600 s = 10 m/s
24.q Convert 100 km/h to m/s. Round to 1 decimal place.
24.a 27.8 m/s
24.e 100 km/h = 100 000 m : 3600 s = 27.8 m/s
