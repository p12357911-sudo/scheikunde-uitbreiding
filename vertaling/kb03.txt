### sk3h-stoffen-KB
heading Substances and materials
0.q Define composite
0.a A material made by combining 2 or more materials
1.q What is density?
1.a A quantity that shows how light or heavy substances are
2.q What is the formula for density?
2.a Density (ρ) = mass/volume in g/cm<sup>3</sup>
3.q What do you call substances that dissolve well in water?
3.a Hydrophilic
4.q What do you call substances that dissolve poorly in water?
4.a Hydrophobic
5.q Define boiling point
5.a The temperature at which a substance changes from the liquid to the gas state
6.q Define solubility
6.a The extent to which substances dissolve in a liquid, for example in water
7.q Define melting point
7.a The temperature at which a substance changes from the solid to the liquid state
8.q Define renewable
8.a Raw materials that replenish themselves, for example crops
9.q Define synthetic material
9.a A material made by humans that you do not find in nature
10.q Define natural material
10.a A material obtained from nature
11.q What are properties of substances?
11.a Characteristics by which you recognise substances
12.q What are properties of materials?
12.a Characteristics by which you recognise a material
13.q Define electrical conductivity
13.a A quantity that shows how well a substance conducts electric current
14.q Define the particle model
14.a A model in which all properties of a substance are determined by the smallest particles of that substance
15.q What are the smallest particles of a substance?
15.a Molecules
16.q Name 3 characteristics of molecules
16.a They determine the properties of the substance. They move constantly. They attract each other and collide with each other. There is space between them.
17.q Name 3 states of a substance
17.a Solid, liquid, gas
18.q What are raw materials?
18.a Materials that serve as the basis for producing a new substance
19.q Name 3 properties of substances
19.a Specific smell, colour, density, melting point, boiling point, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity
20.q What does a melting and boiling graph show?
20.a The relationship between temperature and time when you heat or cool a substance continuously
21.q What does a melting and boiling graph show about the melting point or boiling point of a substance?
21.a That the melting point and boiling point of a substance have a specific value, at which the substance changes from one state to the other
22.q What is the boiling point of water?
22.a 100 °C
23.q What is the melting point of water?
23.a 0 °C
24.q What is meant by the macro level in chemistry?
24.a Properties of substances that you can observe with your senses, for example smell, colour, state of a substance
25.q What is meant by the micro level in chemistry?
25.a The smallest particles of a substance, which can explain the macro properties of a substance
26.q Define a pure substance
26.a At the micro level it consists of 1 type of particle/molecule
27.q Define a mixture
27.a At the micro level it consists of several types of particles/molecules
28.q Describe the smallest particles in a substance
28.a They determine the properties of the substance. They move constantly. They attract each other and collide with each other. There is space between them.
29.q How do you explain changes of state at the micro level?
29.a The distance and attraction between the molecules change, so that they form a solid, liquid or gas
30.q How do you explain the difference between the solid and the gas state at the micro level?
30.a In the solid state the molecules stay in place and only vibrate on the spot. In a gas the particles move around each other with space between them and collide with each other.
31.q How do you explain the liquid state at the micro level?
31.a The molecules are close together but can move around each other, so that at the macro level the substance can flow
32.q How does a pure substance differ from a mixture when you look at boiling and melting points?
32.a Mixtures show a melting and boiling range instead of a melting point and boiling point
33.q What is a suspension?
33.a A mixture of a solid or a liquid in a liquid with which it does not mix well.
34.q How does a solution differ from a suspension?
34.a In a solution a liquid, solid or gas mixes well with the solvent, giving a homogeneous, clear mixture
35.q Define homogeneous mixture
35.a Evenly distributed substances in which the different substances cannot be told apart
36.q Define heterogeneous mixture
36.a A mixture in which the different substances can still be told apart, with the eye or with a microscope
37.q What is an emulsion?
37.a A cloudy mixture of two liquids in which droplets of liquid form. Each droplet consists of many molecules
38.q What is smoke?
38.a A fine solid spread out in a gas
39.q What is mist?
39.a A mixture of small liquid droplets in a gas
40.q What is a gas mixture?
40.a It consists of two or more gases. Gases always mix well and homogeneously.
41.q What is a solvent?
41.a A liquid in which other substances are dissolved
42.q Name at least 6 separation methods
42.a Adsorption, settling (sedimentation), centrifuging, distillation, extraction, filtration, evaporation
43.q What is meant by separating in chemistry?
43.a Splitting a mixture into separate substances
44.q Name a separation method that is based on boiling point
44.a Distillation, evaporation
45.q Name a separation method that is based on density
45.a Settling (sedimentation), centrifuging
46.q For which separation method is particle size decisive?
46.a Filtration
47.q Name an example of an extraction method
47.a Making coffee or tea
48.q What is left behind on a filter or in a distillation flask?
48.a The residue
49.q A bar of 250 g contains 160 g of cocoa. What is the mass percentage of cocoa?
49.a mass % = mass (g)/total mass (g) ∙ 100% = 160/250 ∙ 100% = 64.0%
50.q How many mL is one L?
50.a 1 L = 1000 mL
51.q A drink contains 20 g of sugar per 250 mL. What is the concentration of sugar in g/L?
51.a concentration = mass of sugar/volume of drink = 20 g/0.25 L = 80 g/L
### sk3h-voeding-verzorging-KB
heading Food and personal care
0.q Which three main groups of nutrients provide energy?
0.a Carbohydrates, fats and proteins
1.q Which nutrient is mainly used as a building material for muscles?
1.a Proteins
2.q Which nutrient is the body's most important short-term source of energy?
2.a Carbohydrates
3.q Which substances does a fat consist of?
3.a Glycerol and fatty acids
4.q Which nutrient provides the most energy per gram?
4.a Fat
5.q What do proteins consist of?
5.a Amino acids
6.q What does starch consist of?
6.a Long chains of glucose
7.q A food contains 20 g of fat. Fat provides 38 kJ per gram. How much energy does the fat provide?
7.a 760 kJ
8.q A product contains 50 g of carbohydrates. Carbohydrates provide 17 kJ per gram. How much energy is that?
8.a 850 kJ
9.q A product contains 10 g of fat (38 kJ per gram), 30 g of carbohydrates (17 kJ per gram) and 5 g of protein (17 kJ per gram). How much energy does this product provide?
9.a 975 kJ
10.q How do you test for starch?
10.a With iodine solution: blue-black
11.q How do you test for glucose?
11.a With Fehling's or Benedict's solution and heating: red precipitate
12.q How do you test for protein with the biuret test?
12.a With sodium hydroxide solution and copper sulfate: purple colour
13.q How do you test for fat?
13.a A grease spot on paper: the paper becomes translucent
14.q What does your body need minerals such as calcium for?
14.a For strong bones and teeth
15.q What is an important function of vitamins?
15.a They help the body to work properly, in small amounts
16.q Which substance is an example of a preservative in food?
16.a Salt
17.q Why do you preserve food?
17.a To prevent bacteria and moulds from spoiling it
18.q Which method does not use a chemical additive to preserve food?
18.a Freezing
19.q Why does a cut apple turn brown?
19.a The substances in the apple react with oxygen from the air
20.q Which substance prevents browning caused by oxygen (antioxidant)?
20.a Vitamin C
21.q What does a pH of 3 mean?
21.a The solution is acidic
22.q What is the pH of a neutral solution?
22.a 7
23.q Which pH belongs to an alkaline solution?
23.a 10
24.q What colour does blue litmus have in an acidic solution?
24.a Red
25.q Which substance in lemon juice makes it acidic?
25.a Citric acid
26.q You dilute an acidic solution with water. What happens to the pH?
26.a The pH becomes higher and gets closer to 7
27.q Which reaction equation of hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide solution is correct?
27.a HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H<sub>2</sub>O
28.q What is formed in a neutralisation reaction of an acid and a base?
28.a A salt and water
29.q What is an emulsion?
29.a A mixture of two liquids that do not dissolve in each other, such as oil and water
30.q Which substance keeps oil and water mixed in mayonnaise?
30.a An emulsifier such as egg yolk
31.q What do you call the part of a soap molecule that dissolves in water?
31.a Hydrophilic head
32.q What do you call the part of a soap molecule that dissolves well in fat?
32.a Hydrophobic tail
33.q Why can soap remove a grease stain?
33.a The hydrophobic tails attach to the fat and the hydrophilic heads to the water
34.q Explain why you cannot get oil out of your clothes with water alone
34.a Oil is hydrophobic and does not dissolve in water. Soap has a hydrophobic tail that attaches to the oil and a hydrophilic head that dissolves in water
