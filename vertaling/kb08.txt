### sk4h-koolwaterstoffen-KB
heading Hydrocarbons
0.q What are hydrocarbons?
0.a Substances that consist only of carbon and hydrogen
1.q What is the general formula of the alkanes?
1.a C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+2</sub>
2.q What is the general formula of the alkenes (with one double bond)?
2.a C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n</sub>
3.q What is the molecular formula of hexane?
3.a C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>14</sub>
4.q What is the molecular formula of propene?
4.a C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>6</sub>
5.q Which name belongs to C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub>?
5.a Butane
6.q Which name belongs to C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>12</sub>?
6.a Pentane
7.q How many hydrogen atoms does an alkane with 7 carbon atoms have?
7.a 16: C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+2</sub> with n = 7: 2 × 7 + 2 = 16 (heptane, C<sub>7</sub>H<sub>16</sub>)
8.q What does saturated mean for a hydrocarbon?
8.a There are only single bonds between the carbon atoms
9.q What is the characteristic of an alkene?
9.a A double bond between two carbon atoms (C=C)
10.q How do you show that a substance is an alkene?
10.a Bromine water is decolourised
11.q What are isomers?
11.a Substances with the same molecular formula but a different structural formula
12.q Which substance is an isomer of butane?
12.a 2-methylpropane: this also has the formula C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub>
13.q How many isomers does C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>12</sub> have?
13.a 3: pentane, 2-methylbutane and 2,2-dimethylpropane
14.q What is the name of the substance with the formula C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH?
14.a Ethanol
15.q What is a cycloalkane?
15.a For example cyclohexane, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>
16.q Which substances are formed in the complete combustion of a hydrocarbon?
16.a Carbon dioxide and water
17.q Which substances can be formed in the incomplete combustion of a hydrocarbon?
17.a Carbon monoxide, soot (carbon) and water
18.q What is the reaction equation for the complete combustion of methane?
18.a Step 1 (in words): methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Step 2 (formulae): CH<sub>4</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 3 (atom balance): CH<sub>4</sub> + 2 O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + 2 H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 4 (simplifying): Not needed: the coefficients are already the smallest whole numbers. Reaction equation: CH<sub>4</sub>(g) + 2 O<sub>2</sub>(g) → CO<sub>2</sub>(g) + 2 H<sub>2</sub>O(l)
19.q What is the reaction equation for the complete combustion of propane?
19.a Step 1 (in words): propane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Step 2 (formulae): C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 3 (atom balance): C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub> + 5 O<sub>2</sub> → 3 CO<sub>2</sub> + 4 H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 4 (simplifying): Not needed: the coefficients are already the smallest whole numbers. Reaction equation: C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub>(g) + 5 O<sub>2</sub>(g) → 3 CO<sub>2</sub>(g) + 4 H<sub>2</sub>O(l)
20.q What is the reaction equation for the complete combustion of butane?
20.a Step 1 (in words): butane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Step 2 (formulae): C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 3 (atom balance): C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> + 6½ O<sub>2</sub> → 4 CO<sub>2</sub> + 5 H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 4 (simplifying): Multiply by 2: 2 C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> + 13 O<sub>2</sub> → 8 CO<sub>2</sub> + 10 H<sub>2</sub>O. Reaction equation: 2 C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub>(g) + 13 O<sub>2</sub>(g) → 8 CO<sub>2</sub>(g) + 10 H<sub>2</sub>O(l)
21.q What is the reaction equation for the complete combustion of ethene?
21.a Step 1 (in words): ethene + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Step 2 (formulae): C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 3 (atom balance): C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub> + 3 O<sub>2</sub> → 2 CO<sub>2</sub> + 2 H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 4 (simplifying): Not needed: the coefficients are already the smallest whole numbers. Reaction equation: C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>(g) + 3 O<sub>2</sub>(g) → 2 CO<sub>2</sub>(g) + 2 H<sub>2</sub>O(l)
22.q What is the reaction equation for the complete combustion of ethanol?
22.a Step 1 (in words): ethanol + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Step 2 (formulae): C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 3 (atom balance): C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH + 3 O<sub>2</sub> → 2 CO<sub>2</sub> + 3 H<sub>2</sub>O. Step 4 (simplifying): Not needed: the coefficients are already the smallest whole numbers. Reaction equation: C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH(l) + 3 O<sub>2</sub>(g) → 2 CO<sub>2</sub>(g) + 3 H<sub>2</sub>O(l)
23.q Ethene reacts with bromine. What is the reaction equation?
23.a Step 1 (in words): ethene + bromine → 1,2-dibromoethane. Step 2 (formulae): C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub> + Br<sub>2</sub> → C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>Br<sub>2</sub>. Step 3 (atom balance): C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub> + Br<sub>2</sub> → C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>Br<sub>2</sub>. Step 4 (simplifying): Not needed: the coefficients are already the smallest whole numbers. Reaction equation: C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>(g) + Br<sub>2</sub>(l) → C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>Br<sub>2</sub>(l)
24.q Methane reacts with bromine under the influence of light. What is the reaction equation?
24.a Step 1 (in words): methane + bromine → bromomethane + hydrogen bromide. Step 2 (formulae): CH<sub>4</sub> + Br<sub>2</sub> → CH<sub>3</sub>Br + HBr. Step 3 (atom balance): CH<sub>4</sub> + Br<sub>2</sub> → CH<sub>3</sub>Br + HBr. Step 4 (simplifying): Not needed: the coefficients are already the smallest whole numbers. Reaction equation: CH<sub>4</sub>(g) + Br<sub>2</sub>(l) → CH<sub>3</sub>Br(g) + HBr(g)
25.q What is the name of the reaction in which ethene and bromine combine to form one substance?
25.a Addition
26.q What is the name of the reaction in which an H atom of an alkane is replaced by a bromine atom?
26.a Substitution
27.q Which functional group belongs to an alcohol?
27.a The hydroxyl group (–OH)
28.q Which functional group belongs to a carboxylic acid?
28.a The carboxyl group (–COOH)
29.q What is the formula of ethanoic acid?
29.a CH<sub>3</sub>COOH
30.q Which substances are formed when ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol?
30.a Ethyl ethanoate and water
31.q What is the separation of crude oil into fractions (fractional distillation) based on?
31.a On differences in boiling point
32.q Which substance has the higher boiling point: pentane or hexane? (use BINAS table 42B)
32.a Hexane: the longer the carbon chain, the higher the boiling point
33.q Name two uses of hydrocarbons.
33.a For example fuel (natural gas, petrol) and raw material for plastics
34.q Why is the carbon monoxide that is formed in incomplete combustion dangerous?
34.a It is poisonous: CO binds to the haemoglobin in the blood, so less oxygen is transported
### sk4h-reactiekinetiek-KB
heading Reaction rate and energy
0.q What is an exothermic reaction?
0.a A reaction in which energy is released
1.q What is an endothermic reaction?
1.a A reaction in which energy is absorbed
2.q Is the combustion of natural gas exothermic or endothermic?
2.a Exothermic
3.q Is photosynthesis exothermic or endothermic?
3.a Endothermic (energy from sunlight is absorbed)
4.q What is the activation energy?
4.a The minimum energy needed to start a reaction
5.q What is a catalyst?
5.a A substance that speeds up a reaction and is not used up itself
6.q What does a catalyst do to the activation energy?
6.a It becomes lower
7.q What does a catalyst do to the heat of reaction?
7.a Nothing, the heat of reaction stays the same
8.q What is an effective collision?
8.a A collision between particles with enough energy and in the right orientation, after which they react
9.q Why does a reaction go faster at a higher temperature?
9.a The particles move faster, so there are more effective collisions per second
10.q Why does a reaction go faster at a higher concentration?
10.a There are more particles per volume, so more collisions per second
11.q Why does zinc powder react faster with hydrochloric acid than a piece of zinc of the same mass?
11.a Powder has a larger contact surface
12.q What happens to the reaction rate of gases if you increase the pressure?
12.a The rate becomes greater, because there are more particles per volume
13.q According to the rule of thumb, the reaction rate doubles for every 10 °C rise in temperature. What happens to the rate for a 20 °C rise?
13.a 4 times as great: 2 × 2 = 4
14.q According to the rule of thumb, the reaction rate doubles for every 10 °C rise in temperature. At 20 °C a reaction takes 80 s. How long does the reaction take at 40 °C?
14.a 20 s: 20 → 40 °C is doubling 2 times, the rate becomes 4 times as great, so 80 s : 4 = 20 s
15.q According to the rule of thumb, the reaction rate doubles for every 10 °C rise in temperature. At 50 °C a reaction takes 12 s. How long does the reaction take at 30 °C?
15.a 48 s: 50 → 30 °C is halving the rate 2 times, the rate becomes 4 times as small, so 12 s × 4 = 48 s
16.q According to the rule of thumb, the reaction rate doubles for every 10 °C rise in temperature. At 20 °C a reaction takes 100 s. How long does the reaction take at 30 °C?
16.a 50 s: the rate becomes 2 times as great, so 100 s : 2 = 50 s
17.q According to the rule of thumb, the reaction rate doubles for every 10 °C rise in temperature. How many times as great is the rate at 50 °C as at 20 °C?
17.a 8 times: a rise of 30 °C is doubling 3 times, 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
18.q According to the rule of thumb, the reaction rate doubles for every 10 °C rise in temperature. At 60 °C a reaction takes 5 s. How long does the reaction take at 20 °C?
18.a 80 s: 60 → 20 °C is halving the rate 4 times, the rate becomes 16 times as small, so 5 s × 16 = 80 s
19.q How is the reaction rate defined?
19.a The change in concentration per time (Δc : Δt)
20.q What is a suitable unit for reaction rate?
20.a mol/(L·s)
21.q The concentration of substance A falls from 0.80 mol/L to 0.50 mol/L in 30 s. Calculate the average reaction rate.
21.a 0.010 mol/(L·s): Δc = 0.80 − 0.50 = 0.30 mol/L; 0.30 : 30 = 0.010 mol/(L·s)
22.q In a reaction, 12 mL of gas is formed in 40 s. Calculate the average reaction rate in mL/s.
22.a 0.30 mL/s: 12 mL : 40 s = 0.30 mL/s
23.q How do you make the reaction between magnesium and hydrochloric acid slower?
23.a Lower the temperature
24.q In an energy diagram of an exothermic reaction, where is the level of the products?
24.a Lower than the level of the reactants
25.q In an energy diagram of an endothermic reaction, where is the level of the products?
25.a Higher than the level of the reactants
26.q How do you read the heat of reaction from an energy diagram?
26.a The difference in height between the level of the reactants and that of the products
27.q In an energy diagram the reactants are at 120 kJ, the products at 80 kJ and the top of the hill at 150 kJ. What is the activation energy?
27.a 30 kJ: 150 − 120 = 30 kJ
28.q In an energy diagram the reactants are at 120 kJ, the products at 80 kJ and the top of the hill at 150 kJ. How much energy is released in the reaction?
28.a 40 kJ: 120 − 80 = 40 kJ
29.q The combustion of methane releases 8.9 × 10<sup>5</sup> J per mole of methane. How much energy is released in the combustion of 8.0 g of methane? (M = 16.04 g/mol)
29.a 4.4 × 10<sup>5</sup> J: n = 8.0 : 16.04 = 0.4988 mol; 0.4988 × 8.9 × 10<sup>5</sup> = 4.4 × 10<sup>5</sup> J
30.q The combustion of methane releases 8.9 × 10<sup>5</sup> J per mole of methane. How much energy is released in the combustion of 2.0 mol of methane?
30.a 1.8 × 10<sup>6</sup> J: 2.0 × 8.9 × 10<sup>5</sup> = 1.8 × 10<sup>6</sup> J
31.q What is a dynamic equilibrium?
31.a The forward reaction and the reverse reaction proceed at the same rate
32.q What happens to the reaction rate while the reactants are being used up?
32.a The rate becomes smaller
33.q Give two ways to speed up a reaction.
33.a For example raise the temperature, raise the concentration, use a finer division of a solid or add a catalyst
34.q Explain why a catalyst makes a reaction faster.
34.a A catalyst lowers the activation energy, so more collisions have enough energy to react
