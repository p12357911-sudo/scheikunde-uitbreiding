heading Calculating and measuring
0.q What is a mole?
0.a An amount of substance of 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup> particles
1.q What is the number 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup> per mole called and what is its symbol?
1.a The Avogadro constant (N<sub>A</sub>)
2.q What is the molar mass?
2.a The mass of 1 mole of a substance in g/mol
3.q How do you find the molar mass of a substance?
3.a Add up the atomic masses (u) from BINAS table 99. The number is equal to g/mol.
4.q Give the formula for the number of moles
4.a n = m : M
5.q What is the molar mass of H<sub>2</sub>O? (use BINAS table 99)
5.a 2 × 1.008 + 16.00 = 18.02 g/mol
6.q What is the molar mass of CO<sub>2</sub>? (use BINAS table 99)
6.a 12.01 + 2 × 16.00 = 44.01 g/mol
7.q How many moles is 36.0 g of water (M = 18.02 g/mol)?
7.a 36.0 : 18.02 = 2.00 mol
8.q How many grams is 0.50 mol of NaCl (M = 58.44 g/mol)?
8.a 0.50 × 58.44 = 29 g
9.q How many grams is 0.25 mol of H<sub>2</sub>O? (use BINAS table 99)
9.a 0.25 × 18.02 = 4.5 g
10.q How many moles is 4.0 g of H<sub>2</sub> (M = 2.016 g/mol)?
10.a 4.0 : 2.016 = 1.984 = 2.0 mol (2 significant figures)
10.g0 2.0 mol
11.q How many molecules is 2.0 mol? (use BINAS table 7A)
11.a 2.0 × 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup> = 1.2 × 10<sup>24</sup>
12.q How many moles of H atoms are there in 3.5 mol of H<sub>2</sub>O?
12.a 2 × 3.5 = 7.0 mol
13.q What is the mole ratio in a reaction equation?
13.a The ratio of the numbers in front of the formulae
14.q In 2 H<sub>2</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → 2 H<sub>2</sub>O: how many moles of H<sub>2</sub>O are formed from 3 mol of O<sub>2</sub>?
14.a 6 mol
15.q What is a substance in excess?
15.a A substance that is left over after the reaction
16.q What is a limiting reactant?
16.a A substance that is used up first
17.q Give the formula for the mass percentage
17.a mass of substance : total mass × 100%
18.q There is 30 g of salt in 200 g of mixture. What is the mass percentage of salt?
18.a 30 : 200 × 100% = 15%
19.q How many significant figures does 0.0230 have?
19.a 3
20.q With how many significant figures do you give the answer when multiplying and dividing?
20.a The same number as the number with the fewest significant figures
21.q Write 0.0023 in scientific notation
21.a 2.3 × 10<sup>-3</sup>
22.q How many m<sup>3</sup> is 1 L?
22.a 0.001 m<sup>3</sup>
23.q How many grams is 250 mg?
23.a 0.250 g
24.q What is measurement uncertainty?
24.a The margin within which the true value lies
25.q Which piece of equipment do you use to measure a volume of liquid accurately?
25.a A measuring cylinder (or pipette, burette)
26.q What is the molar mass of CaCO<sub>3</sub>? (use BINAS table 99)
26.a 40.08 + 12.01 + 3 × 16.00 = 100.09 g/mol
27.q What is the molar mass of Ca(OH)<sub>2</sub>? (use BINAS table 99)
27.a 40.08 + 2 × (16.00 + 1.008) = 74.10 g/mol
28.q How many molecules are there in 0.50 mol of CO<sub>2</sub>? (use BINAS table 7A)
28.a 0.50 × 6.02 × 10<sup>23</sup> = 3.0 × 10<sup>23</sup>
29.q How many grams of NaOH (M = 40.00 g/mol) is 0.250 mol?
29.a 0.250 × 40.00 = 10.0 g
30.q In 2.0 kg of water there is 6.0 mg of nitrate. How many ppm is that?
30.a 1 ppm = 1 mg per kg; 6.0 mg : 2.0 kg = 3.0 ppm
31.q You dissolve 0.50 mol of NaCl to make 2.0 L of solution. What is the molarity?
31.a c = n : V = 0.50 : 2.0 = 0.25 mol/L
32.q How many moles is 10.0 g of CaCO<sub>3</sub> (M = 100.09 g/mol)?
32.a 10.0 : 100.09 = 0.0999 mol
33.q How many grams of water are formed from 4.0 g of H<sub>2</sub> according to 2 H<sub>2</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → 2 H<sub>2</sub>O? (M(H<sub>2</sub>) = 2.016 g/mol; M(H<sub>2</sub>O) = 18.02 g/mol)
33.a Step 1: reaction equation 2 H<sub>2</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → 2 H<sub>2</sub>O (mole ratio H<sub>2</sub> : H<sub>2</sub>O = 1 : 1). n(H<sub>2</sub>) = 4.0 : 2.016 = 1.984 mol (do not round yet), so n(H<sub>2</sub>O) = 1.984 mol. m(H<sub>2</sub>O) = 1.984 × 18.02 = 35.75 g = 36 g (2 significant figures)
34.q What does ppm mean?
34.a Parts per million: 1 part in a million, for example 1 mg per kg
### sk4h-zouten-KB
heading Salts
0.q What is a salt?
0.a A substance made up of positive and negative ions
1.q Which bond holds the ions together in a salt?
1.a The ionic bond (attraction between positive and negative ions)
2.q Which types of element does a salt usually consist of?
2.a A metal and a non-metal
3.q What is the formula of magnesium chloride? (Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Cl<sup>-</sup>)
3.a MgCl<sub>2</sub>
4.q What is the formula of aluminium oxide? (Al<sup>3+</sup> and O<sup>2-</sup>)
4.a Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>: charge balanced: 2 × 3+ = 6+ and 3 × 2- = 6-
5.q What is the formula of calcium phosphate? (Ca<sup>2+</sup> and PO<sub>4</sub><sup>3-</sup>)
5.a Ca<sub>3</sub>(PO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>: charge balanced: 3 × 2+ = 6+ and 2 × 3- = 6-
6.q What is the formula of ammonium sulfate? (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> and SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>)
6.a (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>
7.q What is the formula of iron(III) chloride?
7.a FeCl<sub>3</sub>
8.q What is the name of Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub>?
8.a Sodium carbonate
9.q What is the name of KNO<sub>3</sub>?
9.a Potassium nitrate
10.q What is the name of CuSO<sub>4</sub>?
10.a Copper(II) sulfate
11.q What is the name of Ba(OH)<sub>2</sub>?
11.a Barium hydroxide
12.q What is the name of Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>?
12.a Iron(III) oxide
13.q What is the charge of the iron ion in FeCl<sub>3</sub>? (Cl<sup>-</sup>)
13.a 3+: 3 chloride ions give 3-; so the iron ion is 3+
14.q What is the equation for dissolving sodium chloride in water?
14.a NaCl(s) → Na<sup>+</sup>(aq) + Cl<sup>-</sup>(aq)
15.q What is the equation for dissolving calcium chloride in water?
15.a CaCl<sub>2</sub>(s) → Ca<sup>2+</sup>(aq) + 2 Cl<sup>-</sup>(aq)
16.q What is the equation for dissolving aluminium sulfate in water?
16.a Al<sub>2</sub>(SO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>(s) → 2 Al<sup>3+</sup>(aq) + 3 SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>(aq)
17.q What is the equation for dissolving ammonium sulfate in water?
17.a (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>(s) → 2 NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>(aq) + SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>(aq)
18.q How many moles of ions are released when 1.0 mol of CaCl<sub>2</sub> dissolves?
18.a 3.0 mol: 1.0 mol of Ca<sup>2+</sup> and 2.0 mol of Cl<sup>-</sup>: together 3.0 mol
19.q A solution of calcium chloride has a concentration of 0.10 mol/L. What is the concentration of Cl<sup>-</sup> in this solution?
19.a 0.20 mol/L: for every CaCl<sub>2</sub>, 2 Cl<sup>-</sup> are released: 2 × 0.10 = 0.20 mol/L
20.q How many grams of NaCl (M = 58.44 g/mol) are needed for 0.500 L of a 0.10 mol/L solution?
20.a 2.9 g: n = 0.10 × 0.500 = 0.050 mol; m = 0.050 × 58.44 = 2.9 g
21.q What is a precipitate?
21.a A solid that forms when you mix two solutions
22.q Why does solid table salt not conduct electricity?
22.a The ions are fixed in their places in the lattice
23.q Why does a salt solution conduct electricity?
23.a There are freely moving ions
24.q You mix solutions of silver nitrate and sodium chloride. Which substance precipitates? (use BINAS table 45A)
24.a Silver chloride
25.q What is the equation of the precipitation reaction of silver ions with chloride ions?
25.a Ag<sup>+</sup>(aq) + Cl<sup>-</sup>(aq) → AgCl(s)
26.q You mix barium chloride solution with sodium sulfate solution. Which substance precipitates? (use BINAS table 45A)
26.a Barium sulfate
27.q What is the equation of the precipitation reaction in which limescale (CaCO<sub>3</sub>) forms from ions?
27.a Ca<sup>2+</sup>(aq) + CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup>(aq) → CaCO<sub>3</sub>(s)
28.q You mix solutions of sodium chloride and potassium nitrate. What happens? (use BINAS table 45A)
28.a Nothing precipitates, all combinations dissolve well
29.q Which salt is poorly soluble in water? (use BINAS table 45A)
29.a Barium sulfate
30.q What is the equation of the precipitation reaction of iron(III) ions with hydroxide ions?
30.a Fe<sup>3+</sup>(aq) + 3 OH<sup>-</sup>(aq) → Fe(OH)<sub>3</sub>(s)
31.q What is water of crystallisation?
31.a Water that is built into the crystal lattice of a salt
32.q Calculate the mass percentage of water of crystallisation in CuSO<sub>4</sub>·5H<sub>2</sub>O. (use BINAS table 99; M(H<sub>2</sub>O) = 18.02 g/mol)
32.a 36.1%: M(CuSO<sub>4</sub>·5H<sub>2</sub>O) = 63.55 + 32.06 + 4 × 16.00 + 5 × 18.02 = 249.71 g/mol; 5 × 18.02 : 249.71 × 100% = 36.1%
33.q Name two salts from everyday life and their uses.
33.a For example table salt (NaCl) for food, limestone (CaCO<sub>3</sub>) in building materials or fertiliser (NH<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>3</sub>) for plants
34.q What happens to the ions when a salt melts and why does it then conduct electricity?
34.a The ions come loose from their places in the lattice and can move freely, so the molten substance conducts electricity
### sk4h-zuren-basen-KB
heading Acids and bases
0.q What is an acid?
0.a A particle that can donate an H<sup>+</sup>
1.q What is a base?
1.a A particle that can accept an H<sup>+</sup>
2.q What pH does an acidic solution have?
2.a Lower than 7
3.q What pH does a neutral solution have at 298 K?
3.a 7
4.q What is the [H<sup>+</sup>] in a solution with pH = 3?
4.a 1.0 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mol/L: [H<sup>+</sup>] = 10**-pH = 10<sup>-3</sup>
5.q Calculate the pH of 0.010 mol/L hydrochloric acid (HCl).
5.a 2.00: HCl is a strong acid, so [H<sup>+</sup>] = 0.010 mol/L; pH = −log 0.010 = 2.00
6.q Calculate the pH of 0.0010 mol/L nitric acid (HNO<sub>3</sub>).
6.a 3.00: HNO<sub>3</sub> is a strong acid, so [H<sup>+</sup>] = 0.0010 mol/L; pH = −log 0.0010 = 3.00
7.q Calculate the pH of 0.010 mol/L sodium hydroxide solution (NaOH).
7.a 12.00: NaOH is a strong base, so [OH<sup>-</sup>] = 0.010 mol/L; pOH = 2.00 and pH = 14 − 2.00 = 12.00
8.q Calculate the pH of 1.0 × 10<sup>-4</sup> mol/L sodium hydroxide solution (NaOH).
8.a 10.00: [OH<sup>-</sup>] = 1.0 × 10<sup>-4</sup> mol/L; pOH = 4.00 and pH = 14 − 4.00 = 10.00
9.q Calculate the pH of 0.0050 mol/L barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)<sub>2</sub> (a strong base).
9.a 12.00: Ba(OH)<sub>2</sub> → Ba<sup>2+</sup> + 2 OH<sup>-</sup>, so [OH<sup>-</sup>] = 2 × 0.0050 = 0.010 mol/L; pOH = 2.00 and pH = 12.00
10.q What is the relationship between pH and pOH at 298 K?
10.a pH + pOH = 14
11.q A solution has [H<sup>+</sup>] = 2.5 × 10<sup>-4</sup> mol/L. Calculate the pH.
11.a 3.60: pH = −log (2.5 × 10<sup>-4</sup>) = 3.60
12.q How many times as great is the [H<sup>+</sup>] at pH = 2 as at pH = 4?
12.a 100 times: a difference of 2 pH units is 10<sup>2</sup> = 100
13.q A solution of a strong acid has pH = 2.0. You dilute the solution 10 times with water. What is the new pH?
13.a 3.0: the [H<sup>+</sup>] becomes 10 times as small, so the pH becomes 1 higher
14.q You dilute 10 mL of 1.0 mol/L HCl to 100 mL. What is the new concentration?
14.a 0.10 mol/L: n = 0.010 L × 1.0 mol/L = 0.010 mol; c = 0.010 mol : 0.100 L = 0.10 mol/L
15.q What does it mean that HCl is a strong acid?
15.a All HCl molecules donate their H<sup>+</sup> to water
16.q What does it mean that acetic acid is a weak acid?
16.a Only a small fraction of the molecules donates an H<sup>+</sup> (equilibrium)
17.q Which of the following acids is a strong acid? (use BINAS table 49)
17.a HCl
18.q What is the notation for a solution of the strong acid HNO<sub>3</sub>?
18.a H<sup>+</sup>(aq) + NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>(aq)
19.q What is the formula of nitric acid?
19.a HNO<sub>3</sub>
20.q What is the conjugate acid of NH<sub>3</sub>?
20.a NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>
21.q What is the conjugate base of HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>?
21.a CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup>
22.q Which particle can react both as an acid and as a base (ampholyte)? (use BINAS table 49)
22.a HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>
23.q What is the reaction equation for the neutralisation of hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide solution (in ions)?
23.a H<sup>+</sup>(aq) + OH<sup>-</sup>(aq) → H<sub>2</sub>O(l)
24.q Acetic acid reacts with sodium hydroxide solution. What is the equation of the acid–base reaction?
24.a CH<sub>3</sub>COOH(aq) + OH<sup>-</sup>(aq) → CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup>(aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O(l)
25.q Ammonia (NH<sub>3</sub>) reacts with hydrochloric acid. What is the equation of the acid–base reaction?
25.a NH<sub>3</sub>(aq) + H<sup>+</sup>(aq) → NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>(aq)
26.q You neutralise 25.0 mL of hydrochloric acid with 20.0 mL of 0.100 mol/L sodium hydroxide solution. Calculate the concentration of the hydrochloric acid.
26.a 0.0800 mol/L: n(NaOH) = 0.0200 L × 0.100 mol/L = 2.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mol = n(HCl); c = 2.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mol : 0.0250 L = 0.0800 mol/L
27.q How many mL of 0.200 mol/L sodium hydroxide solution is needed to neutralise 10.0 mL of 0.100 mol/L hydrochloric acid?
27.a 5.00 mL: n(HCl) = 0.0100 × 0.100 = 1.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mol = n(NaOH); V = 1.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> : 0.200 = 5.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> L = 5.00 mL
28.q How many mL of 0.200 mol/L sodium hydroxide solution is needed to neutralise 20.0 mL of 0.100 mol/L sulfuric acid (H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>)?
28.a 20.0 mL: n(H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>) = 0.0200 × 0.100 = 2.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mol; each H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> gives 2 H<sup>+</sup>, so n(NaOH) = 4.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mol; V = 4.00 × 10<sup>-3</sup> : 0.200 = 0.0200 L = 20.0 mL
29.q A weak acid HZ has Ka = 1.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup> and a concentration of 0.10 mol/L. Calculate the pH. (Assume that [H<sup>+</sup>] = [Z<sup>-</sup>] and that [HZ] ≈ 0.10 mol/L.)
29.a 3.00: Ka = [H<sup>+</sup>]<sup>2</sup> : 0.10, so [H<sup>+</sup>] = square root of (1.0 × 10<sup>-5</sup> × 0.10) = 1.0 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mol/L; pH = 3.00
30.q Four acids have these acid constants: HA 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, HB 1 × 10<sup>-5</sup>, HC 1 × 10<sup>-8</sup>, HD 1 × 10<sup>-10</sup>. Which acid is the strongest?
30.a HA: the greater the Ka, the stronger the acid
31.q What colour does litmus have at pH = 2? (use BINAS table 52A)
31.a Red
32.q What colour does phenolphthalein have at pH = 2? (use BINAS table 52A)
32.a Colourless
33.q Explain why water is an ampholyte.
33.a Water can accept an H<sup>+</sup> (H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup>) and donate an H<sup>+</sup> (OH<sup>-</sup>), so it reacts as a base and as an acid
34.q What happens to the pH if you dilute an acidic solution with water? Explain.
34.a The pH becomes higher (towards 7), because the concentration of H<sup>+</sup> becomes smaller
