### sk3t-reacties-KB
heading Reactions
0.a The reactants disappear and new substances with different properties are formed
1.q Name two things that show that a chemical reaction has taken place
1.a For example: a gas is formed, a precipitate is formed, the colour changes, it gets warmer or colder, light is given off
2.q Is the melting of ice a chemical reaction?
2.a No, it is a change of state. They remain water molecules
3.q What happens to the atoms during a chemical reaction?
3.a They are arranged differently. No atoms are added and no atoms are lost
4.q What is on the left of the arrow in a reaction scheme?
4.a The reactants
5.q What is on the right of the arrow in a reaction scheme?
5.a The products
6.q You see a gas escaping during a reaction. Is that an observation or a conclusion?
6.a An observation: you can see it. That a new substance has been formed is a conclusion.
7.q Give the word equation for the decomposition of water, with state symbols
7.a water (l) → hydrogen (g) + oxygen (g)
8.q Give the word equation for sodium reacting with water (sodium hydroxide solution and hydrogen are formed)
8.a sodium (s) + water (l) → sodium hydroxide solution (aq) + hydrogen (g)
9.q What is a decomposition reaction?
9.a A reaction in which one reactant is split into several products
10.q What is a synthesis reaction?
10.a A reaction in which one new substance is made from two or more substances
11.q What is decomposition using electric current called?
12.q What is decomposition using heat called?
12.a Thermolysis
13.q What is decomposition using light called?
13.a Photolysis
14.q Which of these substances can you not decompose: oxygen, water, carbon dioxide or glucose?
14.a Oxygen (O<sub>2</sub>), because it is an element. You cannot decompose elements.
15.q Name three substances that you cannot decompose
15.a For example: all metals, oxygen (O<sub>2</sub>), nitrogen (N<sub>2</sub>), chlorine (Cl<sub>2</sub>), neon
16.a With a burning splint: you hear a pop (a popping sound)
17.q How do you test for oxygen gas?
17.a With a glowing splint: it bursts into flame
18.a With a burning splint: the flame goes out, or with limewater: it turns cloudy (white)
19.q Two gases are formed in the electrolysis of water. Which ones?
19.a Hydrogen and oxygen
20.q What is this an example of: CaCO<sub>3</sub> is heated and decomposes into CaO and CO<sub>2</sub>?
20.a Thermolysis
22.q What do you do in step 3 of the step-by-step method?
22.a Set up the atom balance: balance the equation with coefficients
23.q What do you do in step 4 of the step-by-step method?
24.q What do you put in front of a molecular formula to balance the atoms: a subscript or a coefficient?
24.a A coefficient. You must never change the subscript (the small number)

34.a 50 g, because the mass stays the same (law of conservation of mass)

### sk3t-reactiesnelheid-KB
heading Reaction rate
0.q What is the reaction rate?
0.a How fast the reactants disappear and the new substances are formed: how much substance reacts per second (or minute)
1.q What is the reaction time?
1.a How long the reaction takes, from the start until one reactant is used up
2.q A reaction goes faster. What happens to the reaction time?
2.a The reaction time becomes shorter: a faster reaction finishes sooner
3.q What happens to the reaction rate if you raise the temperature?
3.a The reaction rate increases: at a higher temperature the substance reacts faster
4.q What happens to the reaction rate if you lower the temperature?
4.a The reaction rate decreases: at a lower temperature the reaction goes more slowly, but does not stop at once
5.q What happens to the reaction rate if you increase the concentration of a reactant?
5.a The reaction rate increases: more particles per litre, so more collisions
6.q You make a solid finer, for example powder instead of a lump (greater degree of division). What happens to the reaction rate?
6.a The reaction rate increases: a powder has a larger surface area than a lump
7.q In a reaction between gases you increase the pressure. What happens to the reaction rate?
7.a The reaction rate increases: the gas particles are closer together (pressure only has an effect on gases)
8.q What does a catalyst do?
8.a A catalyst speeds up the reaction and is not used up at the end
9.q You raise the temperature. What happens to the reaction time?
9.a The reaction time becomes shorter
10.q You add a catalyst. What happens to the reaction time?
10.a The reaction time becomes shorter: with a catalyst the reaction goes faster
11.q You increase the concentration of a reactant. What happens to the reaction time?
11.a The reaction time becomes shorter
12.q Does the type of substance affect the reaction rate?
12.a Yes, some substances naturally react faster than others (sodium reacts much faster with water than iron does)
13.q Which of these things affects the reaction rate?
13.a The temperature (also: concentration, degree of division, catalyst, pressure for gases and the type of substance)
14.q You see a graph of the amount of reactant against time. The line slopes steeply downwards. What does that mean?
14.a The reaction is fast: the reactant disappears in a short time
15.q Name three factors that affect the reaction rate.
15.a For example: temperature, concentration, degree of division, catalyst, pressure (for gases) and the type of substance
16.q An effervescent tablet dissolves faster in warm water than in cold water. Which factor is responsible?
16.a The temperature
17.q Why does a powder react faster than a lump of the same substance?
17.a A powder has a much larger surface area, so more particles can react at the same time
18.q Small wood shavings catch fire faster than a large log. Which factor is responsible?
18.a The degree of division
19.q Why do you store food in the fridge?
19.a At a low temperature the spoilage reactions are slower
20.q Why is flour dust dangerous in a flour mill?
20.a The dust has an enormous degree of division (a very large surface area). It then reacts so fast with oxygen that it explodes
21.q A wood splinter burns much faster in pure oxygen than in air. Which factor is responsible?
21.a The concentration of oxygen is higher: air contains only about 21% oxygen, pure oxygen 100%
22.q A catalytic converter in a car makes sure that poisonous carbon monoxide is converted quickly. Which factor is used here?
22.a A catalyst
23.q You add a catalyst. How much product is formed in the end?
23.a The same amount as without a catalyst. The reaction is just finished sooner
24.q What is an enzyme?
24.a A catalyst in living organisms
25.q You let 5.0 g of zinc react with an excess of hydrochloric acid, first at 20 °C and then at 40 °C. What is different at 40 °C?
25.a The reaction is finished sooner, the same amount of hydrogen is formed
26.q Magnesium reacts with 2 mol/L hydrochloric acid and with 0.5 mol/L hydrochloric acid. In which solution does the reaction go fastest?
26.a In the hydrochloric acid of 2 mol/L: a higher concentration gives a higher reaction rate
27.q In a reaction 60 mL of gas is formed in 30 seconds. What is the average reaction rate?
28.q Which two ways are useful for measuring the reaction rate when a gas is formed?
28.a Measure the gas volume per time, or measure the decrease in mass per time
29.q Why does the reaction rate usually become smaller over time?
29.a The reactants are being used up, so the concentration becomes smaller
30.q In a graph of the amount of product against time, the line becomes horizontal after a while. What does that mean?
30.a The reaction has finished, a reactant is used up
31.q You measure the reaction twice with the same amount of reactant. Line B rises more steeply than line A. Which statement is correct?
31.a The reaction of B is faster, but B ends at the same height as A
32.q Name two ways to make a reaction go more slowly.
32.a For example: lower the temperature, make the concentration smaller, use a coarser (lumpy) piece or leave out the catalyst
33.q What is the reaction equation of magnesium with hydrochloric acid?
33.a Mg (s) + 2 HCl (aq) → MgCl<sub>2</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g)
34.q You measure the reaction rate of magnesium with hydrochloric acid. Which substance do you use for that?
34.a Hydrogen (H<sub>2</sub>), because you can measure the gas volume

heading Combustion
0.q What is a fuel?
0.a A substance that you burn to get heat (energy)
1.q Name the three things needed to make something burn (fire triangle)
1.a Fuel, oxygen and a temperature above the ignition temperature
2.q What is the ignition temperature?
2.a The temperature at which a substance starts to burn
3.q Which gas from the air is needed for combustion?
4.q What is a combustion called in which enough oxygen is present?
4.a Complete combustion
5.q What is a combustion called in which too little oxygen is present?
5.a Incomplete combustion
6.q Which two substances are formed in the complete combustion of a hydrocarbon?
7.a Carbon monoxide (CO), soot (C), and also water. Sometimes carbon dioxide as well.
8.q What colour is the flame in the incomplete combustion of natural gas?
8.a Yellow
9.q What colour is the flame in complete combustion in a burner?
9.a Blue
10.q Why is carbon monoxide (CO) dangerous?
10.a It is poisonous and you cannot smell it
11.q What is soot?
11.a Fine black particles of carbon (C) that are formed in incomplete combustion
12.q Which two elements are in a hydrocarbon?
12.a Carbon and hydrogen (C and H)
13.q Which three fuels are fossil fuels?
13.a Coal, crude oil and natural gas
14.q What is the molecular formula of methane, the main component of natural gas?
15.q How do you show that water is formed in a combustion?
15.a White copper(II) sulfate turns blue
16.q How do you show that carbon dioxide is formed in a combustion?
16.a Limewater turns cloudy (white)
17.q Balance: CH<sub>4</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O
17.a CH<sub>4</sub> + 2 O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + 2 H<sub>2</sub>O (left 1 C, 4 H and 4 O, right 1 C, 4 H and 4 O)
18.q Balance: C + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub>
18.a C + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> (already balanced: left 1 C and 2 O, right 1 C and 2 O)
19.q Balance: 2 CH<sub>4</sub> + 3 O<sub>2</sub> → … CO + … H<sub>2</sub>O (incomplete combustion)
19.a 2 CH<sub>4</sub> + 3 O<sub>2</sub> → 2 CO + 4 H<sub>2</sub>O (left 2 C, 8 H and 6 O, right 2 C, 8 H and 6 O)
20.q Balance: CH<sub>4</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → C + H<sub>2</sub>O (incomplete combustion with soot)
20.a CH<sub>4</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → C + 2 H<sub>2</sub>O (left 1 C, 4 H and 2 O, right 1 C, 4 H and 2 O)
21.q Balance: C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O (butane, complete combustion)
21.a 2 C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> + 13 O<sub>2</sub> → 8 CO<sub>2</sub> + 10 H<sub>2</sub>O (left 8 C, 20 H and 26 O, right 8 C, 20 H and 26 O)
22.q Balance: Mg + O<sub>2</sub> → MgO (burning of magnesium)
23.q When 16 g of methane burns with 64 g of oxygen, 44 g of carbon dioxide and some water are formed. How many g of water are formed?
23.a Mass on the left = mass on the right: 16 + 64 = 80 g. Water = 80 − 44 = 36 g
24.q At high temperature in a car engine, nitrogen and oxygen react. Which substances are formed?
24.a Nitrogen oxides (NO and NO<sub>2</sub>)
25.q Which gas causes acid rain (together with nitrogen oxides)?
25.a Sulfur dioxide (SO<sub>2</sub>)
26.q What happens to a burning candle under an upturned glass?
26.a The candle goes out, because the oxygen is used up
27.q Which extinguisher do you never use on a fat fire?
27.a Water. It sinks below the hot fat, boils and makes burning fat splash around.
28.q Why does a fire blanket put out a fire?
28.a The oxygen can no longer reach the fuel
29.q Why does water cool a fire?
29.a The temperature falls below the ignition temperature
30.q With which process do you make petrol and diesel from crude oil?
30.a Fractional distillation
31.q Which substance in the air is mainly responsible for the enhanced greenhouse effect?
31.a Carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>)
32.q Why is there more and more CO<sub>2</sub> in the air because of humans?
32.a Because we burn large amounts of fossil fuels
33.q Name two consequences of the enhanced greenhouse effect
33.a For example: the temperature on Earth rises, ice melts, the sea level rises, extreme weather becomes more frequent
34.q How can you make sure there is less CO<sub>2</sub> emission?
34.a For example: use energy more economically, use solar energy and wind energy, drive less, plant trees

17.a Rinsing agent

33.q Why is a factory not allowed to simply discharge waste water into a river?
33.a The waste water pollutes the surface water
34.q What happens to waste water in a sewage treatment plant?
34.a It is purified before it goes back into the surface water
